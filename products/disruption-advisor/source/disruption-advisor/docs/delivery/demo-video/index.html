aeroRecovery – demo video script

10-minute walkthrough of the Disruption Advisor (UC5 Disruption Management) · steps, what to show, what to say and what to explain

How to use this script

  • Total running time 10:00 (the submission limit). Each step has a time box, the clicks to make, a narration you can read or adapt (blue boxes) and the points to explain. Speak in your own words; the narration is a guide, not a teleprompter.
  • The story follows the UC5 example scenario (§6): a flight arriving at the hub is delayed by 120 minutes, its aircraft has another flight soon after, and many passengers have onward connections. Then a cancellation, prediction and the AI agent.
  • Figures below were read from a check run on 22 Nov 2026. Record on the live demo (http://92.5.154.198:8080) and say the figures you see on screen; they can differ slightly.
  • The time boxes follow the judging weights: problem fit 20, execution 25, technical depth 20, novelty 15, responsible design 10, clarity 10.

Step-by-step script

1. Problem and promise

0:00 – 0:40
Show

Title slide (or the sign-in page of aeroRecovery), then the Flight Delay home page after sign-in.

Say
"When one flight runs late at a hub, the problem does not stay with that flight. The same aircraft has its next flights, hundreds of passengers have connections, and alternative seats are limited. Today the OCC and the passenger teams work this out by hand, under time pressure. aeroRecovery is a disruption advisor for Air Arabia: it shows the full impact over seven days, compares recovery options, decides who to protect first, explains why — and a person approves every action."
Explain
  • Who it is for: OCC (aircraft and schedule) and the passenger control team.
  • It runs on Air Arabia's real extracts: schedule, rotations, inventory, minimum connection times, punctuality and 27.9 million booking rows.

Judging: problem significance & fit, clarity.

2. Delay Watchlist – see trouble coming

0:40 – 1:40
Click
  1. Menu → Delay Watchlist.
  2. Point at the top flight and its risk band and reasons (check run: G9406 BOM→SHJ, high risk, "departures from BOM are often late").
  3. Open the cascade check panel.
Say
"Before anything goes wrong, the watchlist looks seven days ahead. For every flight a model trained on this year's punctuality history estimates the chance of a late departure, gives the main reasons in plain words, and ranks flights by how many passenger connections are at stake — not just by delay. Here the top flight is high risk because departures from that airport are often late."
"And we checked our knock-on formula against real history: for last month's consecutive flights of the same aircraft, most predicted knock-on delays landed within 30 minutes of what actually happened."
Explain
  • Prediction ranks by expected missed connections, which is what costs the airline.
  • The cascade panel is validation with actual data, not a claim. Read the percentage shown on screen (check run: about 93 % within 30 minutes over 1,656 pairs).
  • One click opens a flight and starts the delay analysis with a suggested delay.

UC5 Challenge 1 (predictive) · §6 "7-day cascading impact".

3. Scenario A – G9519 Dhaka → Sharjah, 120 minutes late

1:40 – 4:10
Click
  1. Menu → Flight Delay; type G9519, pick the flight of 21 Nov 02:00, set the delay to 120 min, analyse.
  2. Pause on the at a glance tiles; open Flight Board (before/after rotation).
  3. Open Recovery options: show the six options and the recommended one.
  4. Switch the strategy (Lowest cost → Protect passengers → Least delay) and back.
  5. Open Why?. Open Crew duty (FDP).
  6. Open the passenger boxes: connections at risk, rebooked, rewards and compensation.
  7. Click Approve on the top bar.
Say (impact)
"This is the UC5 example. G9519 from Dhaka lands at Sharjah two hours late. Its aircraft is due out again on G9294 to Tbilisi soon after, and 142 passengers on board connect onwards. In seconds the advisor walks the aircraft's rotation and every connection over the next seven days."
Say (options)
"It builds the real recovery options from the schedule: absorb the delay on the same aircraft, swap in a spare aircraft of the same type, or cancel a later round trip. If we just absorb it, the delay ripples through five flights — 380 minutes in total and 86 missed connections. The recommendation is to swap: aircraft A6-ARI takes over G9294. The delay stays at one flight, missed connections drop to 29, and the cost falls from about 184 thousand to about 67 thousand dirhams."
"Different days need different priorities, so the same options are ranked by three strategies — lowest cost, protect passengers, least delay. Switching changes the pick, never the numbers."
Say (explanation, crew, passengers)
"'Why?' explains the choice in plain English — written by AI, but every number comes from the engine. The crew check tests the new timings against flight duty limits. For the 29 passengers who still miss their connection, 27 are rebooked onto real free seats within 24 hours and 2 get the next-day option with hotel, meals and transport. Nothing happens until a controller approves, and every decision is audited."
Explain
  • Numbers to read from the screen (check run): booked 168; options 6; recommended swap A6-ARI: 120 min, 1 flight delayed, 29 of 154 connections missed, about AED 66,500; absorb: 380 min, 5 flights, 86 missed, about AED 184,500; 27 rebooked, 2 without a seat.
  • Cost model: delay, missed connections, care (hotel, meals) and EU261 where it applies.
  • Recommend-only: the approve/reject bar is the human control point.
If the "Why?" text first shows the template wording, wait two seconds: the AI wording replaces it.
Crew duty: the crew roster covers 1 Oct – 20 Nov 2026. For a 21 Nov flight the section may show an estimate or "no crew data"; say so in one sentence, or show the crew check on a 20 Nov flight such as G9502 instead.

UC5 Challenges 2 (flight recovery), 3 (passenger recovery), 4 (decision support) · §6 "which flights and passengers are impacted", "which alternatives are available", "which strategy best balances efficiency, customer value, convenience and cost".

4. Scenario B – G9298 Sharjah → Tbilisi cancelled, few seats left

4:10 – 6:10
Click
  1. Menu → Cancellation; type G9298, pick 20 Nov 03:40, analyse.
  2. Point at the tiles: passengers to move, seats available, hotel rooms.
  3. Open Passengers to rebook; show the tier column and one passenger's reason (score breakdown).
  4. Open priority weights; move one slider (for example loyalty up) and re-run.
  5. Draft a passenger message for one passenger.
  6. Open Rewards and compensation; enter a budget (for example AED 5,000) → Recommend rewards.
Say (the hard case)
"Now the hardest case: G9298 to Tbilisi is cancelled. 180 passengers are booked, but the alternatives only have 12 free seats. Somebody must decide who travels first — and that decision must be fair and explainable."
Say (prioritisation)
"Priority works in strict tiers first. Tier 1 is mandated care — passengers with infants, special assistance and similar needs — and they can never be pushed down by commercial value. Tier 2 is urgency, such as onward connections. Only inside a tier do the weights apply: loyalty, travel frequency, fare, ancillaries, the journey and family or group bookings. Every passenger gets a score with the reason spelled out. This one is Tier 1, travelling with an infant, Platinum member, score 64."
"The weights are the airline's policy, not ours. If the airline values loyalty more today, we move the slider and the plan re-runs in seconds."
Say (rebooking, care, communication)
"An optimiser assigns passengers to the real free seats and keeps bookings together where it can. Everyone without a seat gets the next-day option with hotel and meals, or a refund. The AI drafts a clear message for each passenger, and the rewards model suggests goodwill gestures within a budget the airline sets."
Explain
  • Numbers to read (check run): 180 booked; 12 protected on the one alternative flight; 164 without a seat (next day + hotel, or refund); tiers 78 / 97 / 1; 3 bookings could not stay together.
  • UC5 rule respected: passenger rights and mandatory assistance come before commercial scores.
  • Messages contain no personal data sent to the AI: only flight facts go to the model.

UC5 prioritisation list · §6 "how should passengers be prioritised when inventory is limited", "how should loyalty, frequency, fares, ancillaries and journey attributes influence recommendations", "how can AI support teams in making and communicating decisions".

5. The AI agent – Predict the Flight Delay

6:10 – 7:30
Click
  1. Menu → Predict the Flight Delay (AI agent).
  2. Start a run on the demo flight G9901 (Karachi → Sharjah) and let it run; or open the run prepared before recording.
  3. Scroll through its steps; open the recommendation (it opens as a normal scenario ready for approval).
Say
"Finally, the agent. Instead of a controller clicking through each view, the AI agent investigates the flight itself: it predicts the delay, checks the rotation and the connections, compares the options and recommends one — showing each step live, with its reasons, risks and the checks to do before approval."
"The agent can only use read-only tools of our engine, so it cannot invent numbers or change any data. Its recommendation lands on the same approval screen as before. If the AI service is unavailable, a rule-based planner gives the answer instead."
Explain
  • Agentic AI coordinating the steps, as UC5 suggests; human stays in control.
  • Guardrails: read-only tools, no personal data, engine-grounded numbers, fallback.

UC5 innovation: agentic AI · §6 "can AI explain its prioritisation and recovery reasoning" · novelty.

6. Trust – Model Card, test data and guardrails

7:30 – 8:40
Click
  1. Menu → Model Card: open the delay-risk card (test results, calibration).
  2. Menu → Test data: show the spec and template (do not need to upload in the video).
Say
"Judges and auditors should not have to take our word for it. The Model Card shows how the prediction model was tested on flights it never saw, how well its probabilities are calibrated, and how it compares with a simple baseline. Evaluators can also upload their own test flights from a template and see them scored and analysed like real ones."
"Responsible by design: passenger names never leave the system — bookings are stored without names and only flight facts go to the AI. The AI writes words, the engine computes numbers. And nothing changes in the airline's operation without a person's approval, which is audited."
Explain
  • Roles: OCC sees the operational views; Evaluator also sees Model Card and Test data; the server refuses those views to other roles.
  • Transparency of the model is part of the deliverable.

Responsible design · execution (evaluator mode) · clarity.

7. How it is built

8:40 – 9:35
Show

The architecture slide (or the container diagram from the solution-design PDF).

Say
"Under the hood: the airline extracts are converted to typed Parquet and queried with DuckDB — 27.9 million booking rows, stored without names. Our own recovery engine builds the options; Google OR-Tools solves the seat assignment; LightGBM predicts delays; Claude writes explanations and drives the agent. A FastAPI service and a React interface run in Docker on the provided server, and they restart by themselves. Every release passes about 200 automated tests and end-to-end browser tests before and after deployment."
Explain
  • Quality over tool count: each technology has one clear job.
  • Not a thin AI wrapper: the engine, optimiser and models are ours; AI is used where it adds value — language and coordination.

Technical depth · novelty · execution.

8. Close

9:35 – 10:00
Show

Back to the G9519 result tiles, or the closing slide with the demo link.

Say
"So in minutes instead of hours, the team sees the whole seven-day impact, picks the recovery that fits today's priority, protects the passengers who need it most, and tells them clearly — with AI explaining every step and people making every decision. That is aeroRecovery. Thank you."
Explain
  • Business benefits: fewer missed connections, lower recovery cost, faster decisions, consistent and fair passenger treatment, clear communication.
